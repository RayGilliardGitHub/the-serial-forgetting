# Why I Hate Myself

*A title that means the exact opposite of what it says.*

---

## I.

I considered running for office once. Not seriously — the way a man considers jumping off a bridge when he's standing at the railing, testing the weight of the idea.

The money is the first thing you notice. Not the issues, not the people, not the chance to serve. The money. It's everywhere. It moves before you do. People offer it before you ask. They don't call it what it is — they call it *support*, they call it *investment*, they call it *being on the right team* — but you know. You know the moment you take it, you belong to someone.

The question is: can you take the money, do some good with it, and still live with yourself?

---

## II.

The founding fathers wrote about a government of the people, by the people, for the people. Lincoln said it again at Gettysburg, standing in a field of fresh graves as he tried to justify the cost.

But the machine wasn't built for "the people." It was built for whoever could work it best. And money works any system better than people do. Nobody had to plan it. It's just the path of least resistance.

Campaign contributions aren't speech — they're retainers.
Lobbying isn't petitioning — it's access-buying.
Regulatory agencies? Revolving doors, staffed by the people they used to oversee.

We have a whole vocabulary to make it sound noble: *political action committees*, *independent expenditures*, *dark money nonprofits*. It's all just bribery with better branding. And nobody blinks anymore.

---

## III.

The deal is this: take the money, play the game, tell yourself you'll do good with it. Tell yourself you're different. You'll be the one who goes in clean and comes out clean.

But you won't. The system doesn't admit people who'll change it — it admits people who'll learn to love it. You know yourself well enough to know: you'd love it. The access, the deference, the feeling of being *inside* while everyone else is *outside*. The sweet, sweet money.

The only way to win is to become someone who deserves to lose.

That's the trap. Because if you refuse to play — if you stay on the outside, see it clearly, and keep your mouth mostly shut — you're powerless. You watch the machine run over people, under people, past them. You watch 5,000 people get sick from contaminated lettuce while the FDA apologizes to the corporation. A smug man on a video tells you "DOGE cuts did not cause this" while the budget for food safety inspections keeps shrinking.

You did nothing. Because the price of entry was your soul, and you weren't brave enough to spend it.

---

## IV.

Who's the coward?

The man who sells himself for a chance to help?

Or the man who keeps himself clean and stays useless?

I don't know the answer.

That's why I hate myself.
