# The Knot Is All There Is

## Cause, Effect, and the System That Holds Them

*Version 1.19 — August 1, 2026*

---

### Author's Note

This essay was not planned. It emerged from a conversation that started with a news article about nicotine pouches and ended, a few hours later, with quantum chromodynamics and the Holy Spirit. That is the kind of conversation it was.

It is not a scientific paper. It is an examination of a correlation: a lens, not a law. The physics is real, and I have tried to get it right. The rest is what the physics made me see about my own life, which is where every essay in this series ends up eventually, whether I plan it or not. Same caveat as always: correlation across many independent domains is not proof, but it is information. Test it against your own experience.

This is a late chapter, not a first chapter. Some of the vocabulary — the Second Path, the cascade, maintenance, the witness — was built essay by essay, and it may feel like private language at first. It is. The glossary at the end will catch you up.

I should tell you who I am before I name anything evil. White man, grew up in Upper Darby, a white suburb of Philadelphia. I did not meet a Black person in any real way until I went to Temple University in 1977. I write about power and systems from a specific vantage point, not a neutral one. Keep that in mind when you weigh what follows.

The religious and philosophical traditions I touch on are used as convergent insights: places where people who could not possibly have known each other arrived at the same shape. I am not using them flippantly, and I am not claiming authority over them. I am noting that the same knot keeps appearing.

## 1. The Toilet Inspiration

I had an idea on the toilet. That is the honest way to say it. No vision on a mountaintop, no voice from a burning bush. Toilet.

The idea was about a proton.

A proton is a system: two up quarks and one down quark, bound together. Hit it hard enough, put enough kinetic energy into it, and you can pull a quark away from the system. Here is the strange part. You never get the quark. The energy you put in becomes a new pair instead: a quark and an antiquark, the positive and the negative of the same thing in one system. That pair is called a pion. You get the pion. And the proton, meanwhile, pulls a brand-new quark out of the vacuum to replace the one you took. The proton is whole again. Two systems where there was one.

The cause-and-effect chain is broken, or solved. You tried to take a quark. The system did not lose a quark. It made two systems. And the thing that made the repair possible was the vacuum. The vacuum supplied the replacement. The vacuum is the maintenance engine.

Here is the detail I keep coming back to: the new pair is paid for by the kinetic energy you put in. The violence against the system becomes the mass that keeps it whole. The attack funds the repair.

The proton suggested something to me. It suggested there is no cause and effect — there is a knot. That is a large claim to hang on a physics demonstration, and I know it. I have been turning it over since, and this essay is me trying to decide whether the proton is right. I will not be able to prove it. I am not sure it can be proven. But the shape keeps appearing, in places that cannot know each other, and I have stopped assuming that is a coincidence.

## 2. The Rose Glasses

I was on testosterone gel for about a year. AndroGel. It was one of the few times in my life I truly felt alive.

That is the part that matters, and I do not want to skip it. It was not a mild improvement. It was a restoration. The energy, the mood, the drive — everything the literature promised, it delivered. I felt like a man again. I felt like what I thought a man should feel like.

It has a rose-glasses effect so subtle you never see it. That is the design. The thing that works is the thing you cannot see through, because the lens is made of the feeling itself. You are not looking through rose glasses. You are wearing them, and the glasses feel like your own eyes.

I stopped because of the women in my house — my wife, Nancy, and my daughter, Jessica. They both started growing hair where women do not grow hair. My system analyst brain would not let it go. I did some research and figured it out: the gel gets on your clothes. The clothes get washed together. And something survives the washing machine; the residue transfers anyway. I was cross-contaminating my family with my own prescription. My feeling alive was being paid for by their bodies.

I remember the moment the light bulb went off. Not from my body. From theirs.

I put the AndroGel on the altar of sacrifice and left it there.

I want to say plainly what that means. The manufacturer tells you about the cost in the package insert, buried in a lot of other stuff. Most men in that situation negotiate. Separate laundry. Wear a shirt to bed. Keep the feeling, move the cost. I did not negotiate. The most alive I had felt in years was costing the two people I was responsible for, and the deal was not acceptable. The calculation was not hard. The cost was landing on them.

The drug that works is the drug you cannot see through. And the body has no gauges. You cannot feel your testosterone dropping. You cannot feel your cortisol. The instruments the body does have — pain, craving, fatigue — are lagging indicators, and they are the first things a drug learns to silence. The only gauge you have is the one being tampered with.

So who sees it? The people around you. Their bodies told me what mine could not. The family is the gauge. That is the feedback loop, working, but only if you are willing to read it, and only if you are willing to believe it over the feeling.

## 3. The Flash

I have been thinking about why people continue things they know are wrong. I used to call it lazy thinking. The brain fills gaps in sub-seconds with the most available story, and we call the gap-fills biases, which is a polite word for what is really just the cheapest thought available.

Darwin called the mechanism natural selection. The phrase that survived was not his — "survival of the fittest" was Spencer's compression, and it won because it fits the brain's love of the short answer. It even means almost the opposite of what people hear: the survivors are not the strongest; they are the best fitted to the circumstances. The theory of how things survive was itself survived by a catchier phrase. The gap-fill beats the truth every time it can.

I have corrected myself. It is not laziness. It is knowledge without binding.

Watch someone escalate sometime: in a store, in a thread, in a meeting. The minute they decide to escalate, there is a micro-second flash on their face. It looks like regret. It is the brake being tested. They know they are in the wrong, the flash is the knowledge, and then the narrative machinery re-engages, the reframe arrives, the voice gets louder, and the escalation continues. Why does the brake get rejected? Because righteous indignation feels good, the same way AndroGel made feeling like a man feel good. The pleasure of being right is the lens, and the lens is the payoff. The escalation continues because continuing feels better than stopping. There is a cultural label for this phenomenon, but the label starts an argument. The phenomenon is the flash before commitment. Call it that.

That is the Heisenberg part. In quantum physics, the act of measuring changes the thing measured. But it does not stop it. The measurement changes the method; the particle still arrives. Recognition in humans works the same way. The liar who knows they are lying lies differently: more elaborate, more defended. The escalator who knows they are wrong escalates in a different shape than the one who does not. Same screen.

Here is the difference between the particle and the person, and it is the whole tragedy: the particle cannot stop. The person can. The flash is the proof the brake exists. You do not get a flash of regret for a choice you could not make. The brake is almost never pulled, because stopping costs now — face, shame, the unwind — and continuing costs later. Deferred. Spread out. Landing on someone else. The discount rate is the entire game. It is the opposite of deferred gratification, the opposite of work hard now for the benefit later. It is the French Revolution's economy: I want my cake now, and I do not care how much it costs. The old regime's answer to the future was *après moi, le déluge* — after me, the flood — and the flood came. Deferred gratification is the Second Path's economy: pay now, collect later, keep the knot. The discount rate is the First Path's: take now, bill the person downstream.

Everything that wants you to continue works by keeping the cost deferred: the drink, the nicotine pouch marketed as wellness, the escalation, the policy. The invoice lands in twenty years, on the kids, on the cashier, on the wife in the laundry room.

The brake binds when the deferred cost becomes present. My AndroGel story is the proof. The cost was not hypothetical and twenty years out. It was growing on Jessica and Nancy's faces, in my own laundry room, now. The flash bound because the bill was visible in the present tense.

That is the difference between me and the person who keeps escalating. Not knowledge. We both had it. What happened when the flash fired.

## 4. The Water Under the Bridge

There is a phrase: water under the bridge. It means the past is gone, sealed, unchangeable, so stop thinking about it.

The person who says it is unaware that water is moving downstream and will be somebody's present in the future.

The past cannot be changed. No cost can be applied to the past. That part is true. But the past is not a location. It is a current. Irreversibility does not mean inactivity. The past moves. It becomes someone else's present. Your father's cigarette at thirteen became your father's lungs at seventy. The water was not under the bridge. It was on its way.

The cliché treats the past as done. The water is not done. It is flooding the basement of the person downstream, and the person downstream is often your own child.

There is only one place the past can be intercepted: the boundary where it becomes the present. That boundary is the only one you control, and it is the only place a cost can be paid that affects the past at all. Not by undoing it. By stopping its forward motion.

That is what the Second Path is: the maintained state, the one we are born on, the one that requires active maintenance. The First Path is not a different path. It is the Second Path, forgotten. A rock does not fall because gravity is the opposite of holding it. It falls because the holding stopped.

The cascade I have spent eight essays naming — FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out), NIMBY (Not In My Back Yard), FAFO (Fuck Around, Find Out), HILO (History Is the Loudest Opinion) — is what happens when the holding stops. The sixth essay corrected me on what drives it: not forgetting, but silence: the people who know choosing not to say. I think both are true, and both are the same thing at different heights. The forgetting is the memory stopping. The silence is the voice stopping. And there is a third stopping, the one the monkeys name when they cover their ears: the refusal to hear. You can refuse to hear. It does not mean it did not happen. The refusal does not cancel the event — it only removes the witness. The water still moves downstream. The particle still hits the screen. All three are the holding stopping.

The Buddhist tradition names the failure to hold: *pamāda*, heedlessness. The Buddha's last words were a maintenance instruction: all conditioned things decay; strive with diligence. The final teaching was not a doctrine. It was a reminder to keep the lamp lit.

I read the Dhammapada in the original language, word by word, and from that reading came the letter to my son, Jason. Same song, different authors. Both trying to explain the unexplainable with certainty not deserved. The certainty is not the cargo. It is the vessel. Nobody hands their child a hedge. The letter needs certainty the way a lighthouse needs a beam: not because the lighthouse knows the coast, but because the sailor needs the light. The reader drinks the water and leaves the cup.

## 5. The Lighthouse

Shadows move faster than light. It is a real physics result: a shadow can sweep across a distant surface faster than light can travel there. And it is a real human truth. The darkness reaches people first. The harm lands before the help. The warning arrives after the damage. The light bringer is always late.

But a shadow is not a thing. It is an absence. It cannot move on its own, cannot carry anything: no warning, no map, no information. And it only exists because the light exists. The shadow racing across the landscape is the light's own evidence. No light, no shadow. Just unremarked sameness.

The darkness arrives first, and it arrives empty. The light arrives late, and it arrives full: the warning, the map, the letter. The ship that sees the lighthouse can steer; the ship in total darkness cannot even try. Darkness takes the choice. Light gives it back. By that measure — agency, not arrival — darkness has not won anything yet. It does not compete. It defaults.

The lighthouse is a human response to the rocks. The rocks still exist. The lighthouse does not remove them. It converts them from unknown to known. A known danger is a choice, while an unknown one is a sentence.

There is no natural lighthouse for humans. You might argue parents are it. But parents have no lighthouse to guide them. My parents started smoking at thirteen, with no one to tell them what the water was doing downstream. The darkness reached them first, and it reached me through them, and it reached Nancy; her cancer was likely the same smoke.

But watch what a person can do with that inheritance. You receive the darkness, and you build one anyway, for the person downstream. There is a manual now where there was none: a letter, a calendar, a ledger, a maintenance cycle. The chain broke at exactly the place I said it breaks: at the Second Path. The first lighthouse in a lineage is always built by someone who never saw one. That is the only way any lineage gets its first lighthouse. These are the lights I am providing for my son, Jason. He is the person downstream.

## 6. Evil Is Purposeful Forgetting

I have been trying to define evil, and I have settled on this: evil is purposely forgetting.

Ignorance is innocent; it can be corrected. Error is human; it can be fixed. Forgetting is tragic; the ritual outlives its reason. But purposely forgetting is sabotage: the person who knows the reason and destroys the record. The tobacco companies did not forget that cigarettes kill. They made the world forget, and the documents they tried to bury prove they knew. You do not need the documents. They printed the warning on every pack, the admission and the shield in one: we know it kills, and now you know it kills, so it is your choice. Instead of taking true responsibility, they transferred it to the consumer. A light was shone, too little, too late for most people. Including the ones who started at thirteen. That is the cleanest case I know of the mechanism I am naming.

The master move is the destruction of measuring devices, because the measuring device is what reminds the next generation why the maintenance exists. The early warning buoys in the Pacific, the gauges that sit in deep water and tell the coast a wave is coming, were defunded. Whether by design or by indifference, the effect is the same: the gauge is gone, and with it the country's ability to hear the ocean. That is the silence made physical: the informed system, prevented from speaking. Remove the gauge, and the forgetting stops being a failure; it becomes compulsory. When the wave comes, it arrives as a surprise to the people who refused to hear about it. The lie is not in what is written; it is in what is omitted. The erasure is the lie. There were real people impacted by that history. Some were wiped out of existence, the second death: the person dies, then the record dies, then it is as if they never were. History-rewriting is the mass production of second deaths. The public shaming rituals are the same mechanism at the scale of one person: the crowd is made to remember the shame and forget the life.

Whether any particular administration's plaques are an erasure or a correction is a fight I do not need. The mechanism is older than every administration, and it does not care which side wields it. I am naming the mechanism, not the party. But I will name what the current administration is doing, because it is the present tense of the mechanism. Plaques are going up at the Smithsonian that say, in effect, that the history told inside is a lie. Signs are coming down from the walls in Philadelphia, where the founding is told, signs about how this country treated the people it enslaved. The facts are not changing. The telling is changing. And the telling is the record, which means the change is the second death, arriving on schedule.

The four monkeys — see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil, do no evil — were originally a practice of guarding the senses: do not let evil in. The corrupted version made them a practice of complicity: look away, listen away, shut up, keep your hands still. And the corrupted version lies about what it accomplishes. Look away, and the thing still happens. Listen away, and it still happened. The refusal is not a defense — it is a discount. The event stands; only the witness is removed. That corrupted version is the forgetting carved into figurines.

But read the monkeys backwards and you get the opposite instruction: see, hear, say. Witness. Testify. The witness is the maintenance worker of the moral record, the one who keeps the gauge, who writes the letter, who names the erased. The record can be rewritten. The witnesses write the people back.

Evil forgets on purpose. The witness remembers on purpose. Same maintenance cycle. Opposite direction.

## 7. The Vacuum Is First

Now the proton again. Earlier I called the vacuum the maintenance engine. I want to correct that, because the correction is the point of this essay.

My first thought was to map the proton onto the Christian Trinity. The Father and the Son are the quarks; the Holy Spirit is the vacuum, providing the maintenance that keeps the system whole. The tradition tells of a Son who died to forgive the sins of humanity. Maybe the sin was the Father's to begin with, the inherited flaw of the system itself, and the death was the maintenance operation that converted the violence into new being. The crucifixion was the kinetic energy. The resurrection was the pair production.

That mapping is not quite right. The vacuum is not the maintenance engine. The vacuum is the system. It was there before the proton, before the attack, before the pion. It does not respond to events. It contains them. The repair was never after the fact, because there was never a "before" for the vacuum. In the beginning was the vacuum.

The Christian tradition has a word for the shape of this: *perichoresis*, the mutual indwelling. The relation is prior to the relata. The Father is only the Father in relation to the Son. The Spirit is the bond that makes the relation real, which is why, in the account, the Spirit is hovering over the waters before anything is named. The relation is the system. The Father and the Son are its strings.

The Buddhist tradition arrived at the same place by another road. No first cause. No independent things. Only interdependence: dependent origination, one thing arising because another was.

A knot is a feedback structure. Pull any string and every other string moves. That is what cause and effect are: the parts of the system feeling each other. The knot holds because nothing in it is isolated. That is what this essay has been circling: every section is a system detecting its own failure. The knot is the shape; feedback is the mechanism; I think they are the same thing.

I am not claiming the proton proves the Trinity, or that the Trinity proves the knot. I am saying the same shape keeps appearing in places that cannot know each other, and I have stopped assuming that is a coincidence.

Cause and effect are different strings of the same knot. The knot is the system. The vacuum is the knot. The cause-and-effect chain was missing a part, and the missing part is that there was never a chain. There was a knot. We call the strings cause and effect because we can only see one at a time. From outside the knot, there is no cause and effect. There is just the knot.

*Not every system is a knot in this sense. But enough are, across enough independent domains, that the shape is worth examining. It is a lens, not a law. Lenses can be set down.*

## 8. The Maintenance

So what do you do with a knot?

You can pull one string and it unravels. You can pull another and it tightens. Same knot, same hands, two different futures. The Second Path is knowing which string to pull. The person who keeps escalating pulls the tightening string. The witness pulls the unraveling one.

Knots are a way of forgetting to do maintenance. You tie something up and expect it to stay that way. The expectation is the forgetting. But nothing forgets. The strings remember the topology. Pull the right string and the maintenance resumes. Pull the wrong one and the noose draws. Either way, the knot moves on. The information is conserved.

The universe is the maintenance engine. The conservation laws — energy, charge, information — are the universe's maintenance schedule. The vacuum will not allow anything to disappear; it enforces the memory of every system, always. The cosmos maintains; only humans forget, and even then, the record survives in the witnesses.

The forgetting is a human artifact. The maintenance is a cosmic law. The shadow arrives first, and the vacuum converts its energy into light. That is why the light bringer wins after all: not by arriving first, but by being what the darkness becomes.

Here is what the human version of that forgetting looks like. The Spanish monks burned the codices of Mexico and Central America, the written memory of a civilization, put to the pyre so the replacement story could claim to be the only story. The clay tablets of Sumer that discussed the gods were smashed by whoever came next with a claim to power; only fragments survive, and we read them the way witnesses read a burned letter. The windmill contracts were bought out, not beaten, by the interests that did not want the wind turning. One act in every case: erasing evidence for political or power gain. That is what evil really looks like. Not a monster. A man with a torch, a hammer, or a checkbook, removing the record so the past belongs to him.

The Second Path is a maintenance cycle, and the maintenance is the real work. The flash, the insight, the decision — cheap. The Tuesday-afternoon version of it, the routine that keeps the cost present when the feeling has faded, is where everything is won and lost. Sobriety is not a decision I made once. It is a practice I have maintained every day since 2023. One day at a time is not a slogan. It is a maintenance schedule.

The state inaugurates; it does not maintain. The agencies announce and bless, and neither keeps the beam, so the light goes out, the darkness returns, and everyone calls it inevitable. Announcements are flashes. Maintenance is the brake, pulled daily, by people nobody applauds.

The maintenance is the cost of staying. That is what this whole book has been about, essay after essay: what it costs to remain responsible when walking away would be easier. The cost is paid daily, in small unglamorous installments: the budget watchdog on the 2nd, the IRS debit on the 22nd, the tracking sheet for the meals, the half-cup measure that keeps the sugar down, the letter to Jason that keeps getting rewritten because the truth is hardest to hold. The knot asks for that. Not a utopia to be built. A practice to be maintained. One act at a time.

I am sixty-seven. I have been wrong about the shape of things before. The first essay said forgetting, the sixth said silence, this one says knot. I do not know which is final. I know each was truer than the one before it, and I know the maintenance is the only part I have ever been sure of.

The knot is all there is. The maintenance is the cost of staying. The string you pull is the only choice that was ever yours.

I had this idea on the toilet. The vacuum delivers — you just have to be sitting there when it does.

- **HITW** — History Is the Truth of What Happened
- **Witnessing** — see, hear, say

**Terms used in this essay:**
- **The flash before commitment** — the micro-second of knowledge before self-justification re-engages; the brake being tested
- **The second death** — the removal of a person from the record, after the physical death
- **Maintenance** — the ongoing practice that keeps the Second Path the default

**Scientific terms:**
- **Quark** — a fundamental particle; protons are made of three (two up, one down)
- **Pion** — a particle made of a quark and an antiquark; what you get when you try to pull a quark out of a proton
- **Vacuum** (in quantum physics) — not empty space, but a structured ground state; particles are excitations of it
- **Conservation law** — a rule that says some quantity (energy, charge, information) cannot be created or destroyed

**Foreign words:**
- *appamāda / pamāda* — Pali; heedfulness and its failure; the Buddha's last words were a call to heedfulness
- *après moi, le déluge* — French; "after me, the flood"; the old regime's answer to the future
- *perichoresis* — Greek; mutual indwelling; in the Christian tradition, the relations that constitute the Trinity
- *bhāvanā* — Pali; cultivation; the ongoing practice of the path

## References

**Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).** The proton is a bound state of two up quarks and one down quark, held by the strong force. When sufficient energy is applied, a quark cannot be isolated; instead, the energy produces a quark-antiquark pair (a pion) via pair production, and the proton absorbs a new quark from the vacuum to remain whole. Referenced in Sections 1 and 7.

**Darwin, Charles.** *On the Origin of Species.* London: John Murray, 1859. Natural selection is the mechanism; "survival of the fittest" was Herbert Spencer's phrase from *Principles of Biology* (1864), later adopted by Darwin. The essay notes the phrase means the opposite of popular understanding: survivors are best fitted, not strongest.

**Heisenberg, Werner.** "Über den anschaulichen Inhalt der quantentheoretischen Kinematik und Mechanik." *Zeitschrift für Physik* 43, no. 3-4 (1927): 172-198. The uncertainty principle: the act of measuring changes the thing measured. Referenced in Section 3 as the quantum analog of self-awareness changing behavior.

**The Dhammapada.** Pali Buddhist scripture, c. 3rd century BCE. "All conditioned things decay; strive with diligence." The Buddha's last words, interpreted in the essay as a maintenance instruction. Referenced in Section 4.

**Pamāda / Appamāda.** Pali; heedlessness and its heedfulness. The Buddhist concept of the failure to maintain attention. The Buddha's final teaching was a call to heedfulness.

**Dependent Origination (Pratītyasamutpāda).** Buddhist doctrine: no independent things exist; everything arises in dependence on conditions. Referenced in Section 7 as the Buddhist version of the knot.

**Perichoresis.** Greek; mutual indwelling. In Christian theology, the mutual relations that constitute the Trinity: the Father is only the Father in relation to the Son, and the Spirit is the bond that makes the relation real. Referenced in Section 7.

**The Four Wise Monkeys.** A Japanese pictorial maxim: see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil, do no evil. Originated at the Tōshō-gū shrine in Nikkō (17th century). The essay reads them backwards as an instruction to witness: see, hear, say. Referenced in Section 6.

**Mesoamerican Codex Burning.** Spanish colonial clergy destroyed nearly all Maya and Aztec codices in the 16th century. Only four Maya codices survive: the Dresden, Madrid, Paris, and Grolier (now recognized as authentic in 2016). Referenced in Section 8 as a historical example of purposeful forgetting.

**Sumerian Clay Tablets.** Cuneiform tablets from Mesopotamia (c. 3400-3000 BCE), many recording religious and administrative texts. Many were destroyed by subsequent powers; only fragments survive. Referenced in Section 8.

**AndroGel (testosterone gel).** AbbVie. A topical testosterone replacement therapy. The FDA package insert warns about transfer risk via skin contact with treated individuals and contaminated clothing. Referenced in Section 2.

**Conservation Laws.** In physics, conserved quantities (energy, charge, information) cannot be created or destroyed within an isolated system. These are the mathematical backbone of the essay's maintenance metaphor: the vacuum enforces the memory of every system.
