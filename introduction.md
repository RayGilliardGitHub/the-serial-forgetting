# Introduction

I did not set out to write a book. I set out to talk to someone who could not answer.

Nancy died on April 21, 2022. We had been married since November 18, 1989 — thirty-two years. For a long time after she died I did what I have always done when something breaks: I kept working. I kept the budget, the calendar, the meals. I kept building systems. That is what I am. Forty-five years as a systems analyst, and the one system I could never debug was grief. It does not show up in logs. It does not respond to root-cause analysis. It just runs, in the background, consuming resources, and the longer you ignore it the more of the machine it takes.

The essays in this book came out of a single month. July 2026, more than four years after she died. Something opened. I do not know what to call it — a crack, a leak, a decision. A YouTube video about the collapse of civilizations. A woman demanding a manager in a store. A news story about contaminated lettuce. A video of a porcelain master breaking his apprentice's bowls. Every one of those became a conversation, and every conversation became an essay. By August there were thirteen.

This book is the record of those conversations.

---

The first essay found a pattern. It is not a complicated one. Every complex system — a quantum state, an ecosystem, a marriage, a civilization — starts coherent. Parts aligned, energy flowing, memory intact. Then the remembering becomes automatic, then unconscious, then forgotten. The forgetting shows up as four stages: comparison, territoriality, collision, and the rewriting of the record. FOMO. NIMBY. FAFO. HILO. The cascade runs at every scale, from a woman in a store to the fall of empires to the silence of the stars. The asteroid was punctuation; the forgetting was the sentence.

I believed that when I wrote it.

The second essay turned the lens around. The cascade is not something that happens to us. It is something we do. You are the Karen. I am the Karen. The simple answer is the right answer, and we reject it because it is simple. Our dissatisfaction with simplicity is the engine of forgetting. I wrote that one next, and it made the first one harder to read. That is how this book works. The essays correct each other. Each one is truer than the one before it, and I have stopped assuming the last one is final.

The seventh essay corrected the first again. The mechanism is not forgetting. The information never disappears. It is silenced — by people who see the pattern and calculate that speaking costs more than watching. The silence of the informed is what lets the cascade proceed. The sky is not silent because no one is out there. It is silent the way a room is silent when the people who could speak have learned not to.

The twelfth essay, the latest, goes further. It says there is no cause and effect; there is a knot. Pull any string and every other string moves. The vacuum was there before anything, and the violence against the system becomes the mass that keeps it whole. The maintenance is the cost of staying. I had that idea on the toilet, and I have learned not to be embarrassed about where ideas come from. The first essay said forgetting. The seventh said silence. The twelfth says knot. I do not know which is final. I know each was truer than the one before it, and I know the maintenance is the only part I have ever been sure of.

If this reads like a book written by a man who does not know where it is going, that is because it is. It is a book about a mind updating its model in public. That is the only honest way to build one. The real enemy — the thing that ends systems, ends marriages, ends empires, ends people — is the inability to update the internal model. The book practices what it preaches, chapter by chapter, and the chapters are the evidence.

---

I am a witness, not an advocate. The early essays tried to convince you. The later ones try to tell you what I saw, including the parts that make me look bad. An advocate needs you to agree. A witness only needs you to believe they are telling the truth. By the end of this book, I would rather be believed than agreed with.

Every chapter asks the same question: what does it cost to remain responsible when walking away would be easier? Nancy got sick; I stayed. Jason needs a system that will work when I am gone; I am building it, and staying. My siblings and I ended up in different versions of the same country; I stayed with mine. The institutions we trusted weaken; some people stop staying with the common good. The answer is the true subject of this book: the cumulative price of staying, paid in small unglamorous installments by the people who keep their seat at the table of someone else's need.

There is a vocabulary you will meet in these pages, and it is private at first. The Second Path is the maintained state, the one we are born on, the one that requires active maintenance. The First Path is not its opposite. It is the Second Path, forgotten. A rock does not fall because gravity is the opposite of holding it. It falls because the holding stopped. Below all four stages of the cascade sits silence: the broken feedback loop that lets everything proceed unopposed. Maintenance is the ongoing practice that keeps the Second Path the default — the budget on the 2nd, the IRS debit on the 22nd, the meal tracking, the half-cup measure, the letter to my son that keeps getting rewritten because the truth is hardest to hold. None of it is glamorous. All of it is the cost of staying. There is a glossary at the back that will catch you up if I move too fast.

---

This is not a self-help book. It will not fix you, and it does not promise to. It is not a science book, though it reads the physics honestly. It is not a political book, though it names what the machine does. It is a book about what it costs to stay, written by a man who stayed.

## On repetition

You will notice that some ideas return more than once. "The First Path is not a different path" appears in more than one essay. The cascade gets explained again in more than one place. This is not an editing failure. These essays were written over a single month, each one triggered by something in the news, and each one was written to stand alone. Any one of them might have been the first thing you read. So each one re-states the framework, the way you give the same directions again to a new driver.

Read front to back, that means the same ideas come around again. I am not going to cut them out, because the repetition is the process. The same question asked again. The same pattern noticed again. Each time a little differently, each time a little more truly. The first essay said forgetting. The seventh said silence. The twelfth says knot. If a sentence feels familiar, that is not a mistake. It is the one thing this book is actually about: a man noticing the same shape again and again, and slowly learning to see it for what it is.

I wrote it for my children first. For Jessica, who asked me for my story — nobody had ever asked before, and her asking is the reason this book exists in any form. For Jason, who is the reason I am still building systems: a manual, a calendar, a ledger, a maintenance cycle that will hold when I am gone. The philosophy in these pages is in the service of that, not the other way around. After them, for the public I will never meet. If one person reads this book and says "Hum," the writing was worth it. I do not need agreement. I do not need applause. I needed to say it, and perchance somebody will read.

The working title of this book is "I was here and I left a mark." The full title, as I first said it, adds a self-correction: "and it is not the shit stain in my underwear." I am keeping both. The first part is the claim every essay makes: I was here. I stayed. I left a mark. The second part is the same claim, immediately corrected by the man who refuses to take himself too seriously. That is the whole book in one line: a declaration of existence, and the refusal to dress it up.

Read them in order. The corrections are part of the argument, and the vocabulary builds as it goes. Skip nothing. The book is short, and the shortest essay in it is the one that tells the most truth: the title means the opposite of what it says.
