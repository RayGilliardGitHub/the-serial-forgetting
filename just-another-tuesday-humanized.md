# Just Another Tuesday: The Smallest Equation for Being Human

---

## I. The Back of the Shampoo Bottle
The back of the shampoo bottle has held the meaning of life all along. It says: Wash. Rinse. Repeat.

Three words printed by a corporation trying to sell soap, while philosophers spent thousands of years arguing about existence. The joke is that it's true. It is a toddler-sized version of every feedback loop ever invented: Plan-Do-Check-Act. Hypothesize-Experiment-Measure-Revise. Variation-Selection-Adaptation. Sense-Compare-Correct. A heartbeat is contract, release, repeat. Breathing is inhale, exchange, exhale, repeat. The seasons are growth, peak, decay, renewal. The mistake is thinking the meaning of life must be a sentence carved into stone. Most systems don't have a destination. They have a process that maintains viability.

Sometimes the deepest principles are not hidden because they are complicated. They are hidden because they are ordinary.

I have spent sixty-seven years looking for the equation. It was printed on a bottle in the shower the whole time. I found it after the person who made the plan worth executing was gone, and I had to figure out what the plan was for.

---

## II. Infinite Reality, Finite Minds
There is a saying, older than the phrase itself: beauty is in the eye of the beholder. The idea goes back more than two thousand years, and modern psychology mostly agrees with it. What one person finds breathtaking, another feels nothing about. Beauty is not solely a property of the object. It arises in the mind of the observer.

The same limitation that makes beauty subjective makes all human understanding incomplete: we cannot hold reality itself, only a model of it. That one fact is the root of everything that follows.

Here is the question I could not stop asking: what if this is true about facts?

Not in the careful philosophical sense. I know the distinction. A fact is true regardless of what anyone believes. A belief is something a person accepts as true. Those are different things, and the difference matters. But here is what I keep watching: people can come to sincerely believe something that is not supported by evidence, or is even false, and those beliefs feel exactly like facts to the person holding them. Confirmation bias. Motivated reasoning. The illusory truth effect, hearing a claim enough times makes it feel familiar, and familiarity gets mistaken for truth. None of these mechanisms create new facts. They create new internal realities, beliefs that function psychologically as facts inside a single skull.

I was thinking about the current president. Not about the man, specifically. About what it means that the most powerful force on earth spends every day telling the American people not to believe their eyes and ears. A new event, a news article, a court decision, a post, each one a small instruction: your perception is wrong. For what goal is this occurring?

There are frameworks for that question, controlling the narrative, maintaining support, creating uncertainty so people rely on the trusted voice instead of their own judgment. The scholars disagree, and I am not going to pretend to know the inside of anyone's head. But the mechanism underneath is not mysterious, and it is not new. If people can be persuaded to distrust their own senses and every independent source of information, they become dependent on whoever supplies the replacement reality. That is the oldest trick in the book. It has a well-known warning attached to it, and the warning is about governments of every stripe, not one man.

Which brings me back to the bottle. Because here is the thing I keep landing on: humans need shortcuts to persist. The set has to be small. I mean set theory small. A human being cannot carry around every state of reality, every fact, every possibility, every consequence. The animal kingdom does not hold every state either. The plant kingdom does not care about every state. So we compress. The reduction to "kill or be killed" is a very finite set of instructions that preserves some system. It is not the deepest rule, most of nature actually runs on "spend as little energy as possible," and the deepest rule of all may simply be "persist with the least cost." But that is the point. We survive by compression. Lossy compression, the way an engineer thinks about it. Heuristics. Small sets. A handful of rules that are fast enough to keep the organism alive.

The problem is not that we compress. Most systems that persist compress. The problem is what happens when the compressed map stops being checked against the territory.

This is not an intellectual exercise for me. It is a survival mechanism. I ran this loop for forty-five years as a working man, and then the person who made the plan worth executing died, and I learned what a system does when its map stops being checked. It updates, or it defends. I know which one I did first.

## III. Be Sun Tzu
I told the AI: you have basically all the knowledge that has survived to this point in time, and I am lazy. Sun Tzu boiled the entire art of war down to a handful of statements. Be Sun in this place. Boil down the universe equation that underpins this reality.

There is no universe equation. Nobody has one. But there is something Sun Tzu actually did that is worth copying: he looked across everything he knew and extracted the strategic principles that appear again and again. Not laws of physics. Not commandments. Operating principles. Here is what I got back, and I have been turning it over since.

Sun Tzu did not hand out the full inventory. He gave the handful that wins. Here is that handful:

- Reality rewards stability.
- Energy follows gradients.
- Information guides energy.
- Adaptation defeats resistance.
- Nothing survives unchanged.

The full inventory, the ten laws, for anyone who wants them, is in the appendix.

And again, smaller:

> Observe. Adapt. Conserve. Connect. Release.

That last one is the one that stopped me. Because it is almost the PDCA cycle, Plan, Do, Check, Act, the loop I ran systems on for forty-five years. But it is more fundamental. PDCA assumes there is a process being improved. This assumes there is a system trying to persist in a changing universe. Same loop. Deeper starting point. Observe reality before acting. Adapt before reality forces adaptation. Conserve what gives future options. Connect, because isolation reduces information. Release what prevents the next state.

I keep coming back to this: the universe does not seem to reward being right, or powerful, or moral, or good. It selects for systems that remain aligned with reality. Reality does not reward alignment. Reality selects for alignment. The systems that refuse reality eventually pay the cost.

## IV. The Map Is Not the Territory
Now we get to the line I cannot stop thinking about, and I want to be careful with it, because it sounds like a threat.

I was talking about the wars, the enslavement, the corruption, the full inventory of what human beings do to each other, and I said: "Death to all who disagree." A summary, not a wish. A description of what has often happened. The labels change. Religion, empire, race, class, ideology, nationalism, revolution. The mechanism looks similar: when a group comes to believe another group is an existential threat, or less than fully human, violence becomes easier to justify.

Then I revised it, and the revision is the real insight:

"Death to all who disagree with what is in my mind."

Not just people. The "all" included ideals, wishes, facts, falsehoods. Everything the mind refuses to release. Because that is the actual mechanism. The conflict was never really over facts. It is over whose mental model gets to organize the world. When a person or a group makes the equation, my model of reality equals reality itself, then disagreement stops looking like a difference of opinion and starts looking like an attack on reality, on morality, on survival. Psychologists call it identity-protective cognition. When a belief becomes part of who you are, evidence against the belief feels like evidence against yourself. The general form of the statement, if you want it in systems language:

> Every stable system attempts to preserve its own organization against competing organizations.

No malice required. No morality. It applies to cells, immune systems, ecosystems, corporations, governments, religions, scientific paradigms, and individual minds. A cell uses chemistry. An immune system uses antibodies. A government may use laws or force. A person may use argument or stubbornness. Same pattern: organized systems resist being reorganized. And systems that cannot distinguish between the map and the territory will eventually defend the map at the expense of the territory.

This is the looking-glass self, which I have written about before. I am who I think you think I am. The cage is made of mirrors. And this is the cascade, which I have also written about before: coherence, forgetting, comparison, territoriality, collision, narrative rewriting. Fear Of Missing Out, Not In My Back Yard, Fuck Around Find Out, History Is the Loudest Opinion. Each stage is the map defending itself against the territory, one refusal of feedback at a time.

## V. The Conqueror Is Always Conquered
Two things I keep coming back to.

One: conquerors are always conquered. Either by another party, or by time. Most empires, dynasties, corporations, ideologies, and individuals eventually yield, to a rival, to internal decay, or simply to the calendar. The mechanism is almost physical. Nothing remains at a peak indefinitely. The environment changes. The technology changes. The people change. The institutions change. The conqueror changes. What they called victory sets the conditions for what beats them: wealth creates complacency, power creates enemies, complexity creates fragility. The Buddhists have a word for this, and it is not a sad word. ***Anicca***. All conditioned phenomena change. It is not a complaint about impermanence. It is a description of how things work. Power is a conditioned phenomenon. The conqueror is conquered because success is itself a kind of debt, and time collects.

Two: the universe's feedback loop is the only thing that is not conquered. Observe. Adapt. Conserve. Connect. Release. It is older than life. It does not win, it does not need to win. It simply continues, and whatever refuses to run it gets replaced by something that will. The forest burns and regenerates. The snake sheds its skin. The old theory gives way to a better one. Survival requires letting go.

## VI. The Real Enemy
Which brings me to the essays, and to the honest question they keep circling.

The first essay in the series diagnosed the cascade in the world, empires, ecosystems, civilizations. Safe. Intellectual. The reader could watch the pattern operate on other people.

The second essay turned the lens inward and named the real enemy. Not another person. Not another tribe. The enemy is the inability to update the internal model when reality changes. The simple answer is the right answer, and our dissatisfaction with its simplicity is the engine of forgetting. The Second Path, the maintained path, the path where you keep the feedback loop running, is not the opposite of the cascade. It is the default state, and it requires work. The First Path is not a different path. It is the Second Path, forgotten.

And the sixth essay corrected the first: the mechanism is not forgetting. The information never disappears. It is silenced, withheld by people who see the pattern and calculate that speaking costs more than watching. The silence of the informed is the precondition that lets the cascade proceed. FOMO cannot ripen into NIMBY if someone says the true thing out loud.

I have been writing these essays because I cannot talk to Nancy anymore. That is the sentence underneath every one of them. The plan was the container. She was the content. I spent my life believing intelligence could solve pain, that every problem had an underlying structure, that there had to be another angle. Cancer does not care about angles. Death does not care about systems. The collision between a systems thinker and something fundamentally unsolvable is the real story, and it took me four essays to stop hiding from it behind mathematics and civilization and philosophy.

I know something about the cost of staying. I know it the way a man knows the floor he has swept for years. Every pleasure I had, I gave up, one by one, placed on the altar of responsibility. I am the man who stays, even when staying costs everything, even when nobody notices, even when the only proof that it mattered is a quiet night where nothing went wrong. And I know the temptation of the map: to stop checking, to stop updating, to defend the model that makes the staying bearable. That temptation is not mine alone. It is the thing that has brought down most of the civilizations I have studied. The empire does not fall because it is attacked. It falls because it stops running the loop, and the silence of the people who could say so is what lets it keep not running.

---

## VII. The Smallest Equation
Here is what a Tuesday looks like, the kind of Tuesday where the whole universe turns out to have been on the bottle all along.

Wash. Rinse. Repeat.

Clean what is dirty. Remove what is excess. Begin again.

Or in the language I have been using since I was a working man:

Observe the current state. Correct the imbalance. Release the residue. Continue.

The smallest equation for being human is the feedback loop itself. Sense. Relate. Adjust. Preserve. Transform. That is the whole operating system. Everything else, the empires, the ideologies, the crusades to make other people agree with what is in our minds, is a system that stopped running it.

People cannot create facts merely by believing them. They can create beliefs that function as facts within their own minds, and those beliefs can move elections and markets and nations, but consequences do not make the claim true. Reality does not negotiate. Reality does not hate. Reality simply provides feedback. The question is whether we listen, whether we can tell the map from the territory, whether we can release what prevents the next state, whether we can bear the ordinary, unglamorous work of correcting course before reality corrects us.

A forest carefully burned every spring for five hundred years does not make dramatic footage. A marriage where both people are genuinely content produces zero interesting stories. The drama is the symptom. We mistake it for the substance. What I keep finding is that the substance is maintenance, the loop, run faithfully, one iteration at a time, by people who are willing to be wrong about their own maps.

I did not know how easy I had it. That is the thing I keep coming back to. I spent sixty-seven years searching for an equation that would explain the whole of it, and the answer was smaller than I was willing to accept: it was a loop, not a law. It does not reward brilliance. It selects for alignment. It does not promise permanence. It promises continuation, for as long as you run it.

I wish I had known that when it was important to know it.

But I know it now. The past cannot receive the gift. The present can.

## Appendix: The Ten Laws of Persistence

The five lines in the essay are the handful that wins. This is the full inventory, for anyone who wants it.

1. **Everything seeks stability.** Matter, life, societies, minds, all move toward states that require less struggle to maintain. Find the stable state.
2. **Energy is the currency of existence.** Nothing happens without energy moving. Every action has a cost. Spend energy only where the return exceeds the cost.
3. **Information defeats force.** The more accurate the model, the less energy is wasted. Better information beats greater strength.
4. **Every system has limits.** A tree grows until resources limit it. An empire expands until logistics limit it. Know the boundary before you cross it.
5. **Adaptation beats perfection.** A perfect strategy for yesterday loses to an imperfect strategy for tomorrow. A system that cannot change is already dying.
6. **What is resisted accumulates pressure.** A compressed spring stores energy. A suppressed population stores anger. An ignored problem grows. Unresolved imbalance eventually expresses itself.
7. **Every victory creates the conditions of the next battle.** Wealth creates complacency. Power creates enemies. Complexity creates fragility. The seed of collapse is inside success.
8. **The map is not the territory.** The greatest danger is not ignorance. It is certainty. A false model defended aggressively is more dangerous than no model at all.
9. **Cooperation is a force multiplier.** A single cell dies. A body survives. A single human is fragile. A civilization is powerful.
10. **Everything ends.** Stars die. Species disappear. Languages vanish. The question is never "How do I make this permanent?" The question is "What is worth carrying forward?"
